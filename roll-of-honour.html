Roll of Honour: Those Who Served

A permanent record of Brampton people represented in Old Brampton Churchyard research and local wartime memorials, honouring military, nursing and support service.

Photo: Brampton Old Church Cemetery by Alan Sawyer, CC BY-NC-ND 2.0

This Roll of Honour brings together the stories of men and women connected with Brampton’s wartime memorials and Old Brampton Churchyard. It includes those buried in the churchyard, those commemorated on local memorials, and Brampton people whose graves or official memorials lie elsewhere.

Entries are arranged by conflict or period of service. Each card gives a short account; select Read full biography for the fuller story and the sources used. Where one person served in more than one conflict, separate profiles are linked so that their service can be followed across the page.

First World War (1914–1918)

People connected with Brampton who served during the First World War, including those buried locally and those commemorated on the town and church memorials.

Major William Symington

Major / Surgeon-Major, 4th Battalion Border Regiment · died 3 September 1916, aged 51

A Brampton doctor for almost three decades, William Symington combined a long medical career with service as a Surgeon-Major in the Border Regiment. Mobilised in August 1914, he served as a medical officer until illness led to his death at Brampton in September 1916.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference A133

Burial / commemoration Brampton Old Churchyard.

Lieutenant Lawrence Stanley Carrick

Lieutenant, 49th Battalion Canadian Infantry (Alberta Regiment), service no. 1932 · died 15 September 1916, aged 34

Born into the Carrick family of Brampton, Lawrence Stanley Carrick had already served in the South African War and the Royal North-West Mounted Police before joining the Canadian forces. He was killed at Courcelette on the Somme on 15 September 1916 and has no known grave. See his South African War profile.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference E122

Burial / commemoration Canadian National Vimy Memorial · no known grave

Bugler Thomas Fallowfield “Tom” Mounsey

Bugler / Private, 11th Border Regiment (Lonsdale Battalion), service no. 17605 · died 18 November 1916, aged 33

Tom Mounsey, a mason and native of Hackthorpe, enlisted in February 1915 and served with the Lonsdale Battalion. He was reported missing on 18 November 1916; his family initially hoped he had been taken prisoner, but he was later confirmed dead.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B21

Burial / commemoration Thiepval Memorial · no known grave

Corporal Thomas George “Tom” Armstrong

Corporal, 11th Border Regiment (Lonsdale Battalion), service no. 19591 · died 1 July 1916

Thomas George Armstrong of Beck Side, Brampton, served with the Lonsdale Battalion and was killed on the first day of the Battle of the Somme. A letter to his sister said that he was hit by a shell and died shortly afterwards in the arms of a sergeant.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B28

Burial / commemoration Not stated in the supplied research; commemorated on the family memorial at Brampton Old Churchyard.

Private Mark William Richardson

Private, 6th Battalion Border Regiment, service no. 10895 · died 23 November 1916

Mark William Richardson was the son of Mark and Susan Richardson of Longtown and a grandson of the Brampton Richardson family. He served in the 6th Border Regiment and was killed in action in France on 23 November 1916.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B37

Burial / commemoration Not stated in the supplied research; also commemorated at Longtown.

Second Lieutenant Arthur Hetherington

Second Lieutenant, 9th Battalion King’s Own Yorkshire Light Infantry · died 21/22 March 1918, aged 29

A former Brampton schoolteacher, Arthur Hetherington rose from the ranks to a commission in the King’s Own Yorkshire Light Infantry. He was lost during the German spring offensive in March 1918 when his platoon was caught by machine-gun fire and his comrades were forced to withdraw.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B47

Burial / commemoration His body could not be recovered; no burial location is established in the supplied research.

Lance-Corporal William Frederick Smith

Private / Lance-Corporal, Machine Gun Corps (Infantry), service no. 29360 · died 23 January 1917, aged 22

William Frederick Smith, son of a Market Place boot and shoemaker, was well known locally as a footballer and champion oarsman. He was killed when a German shell passed through the roof of his dugout, killing him and three comrades.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B60

Burial / commemoration Guards’ Cemetery, Combles · grave II.A.15

Private George Taylor

Private, 4th Border Regiment · died 31 March 1915, aged 25

George Taylor of Carlisle Road served with the 4th Border Regiment and went overseas in late 1914. He died on active service at Maymyo in Upper Burma on 31 March 1915, aged twenty-five.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B67

Burial / commemoration Taukkyan War Cemetery, Myanmar · grave 27.J.7

Driver Thomas James “Tom” Taylor

Driver, Royal Army Service Corps; formerly Royal Field Artillery · died 30 June 1918, aged 30

Thomas James Taylor, brother of George Taylor, served in the Royal Army Service Corps after originally enlisting in the Royal Field Artillery. He died of illness at the Royal Herbert Hospital, Woolwich, and was brought home for burial in Brampton.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B66 (military headstone) and B67 (family memorial)

Burial / commemoration Brampton Old Churchyard

Second Lieutenant Joseph Harding

Second Lieutenant, 3rd Battalion Border Regiment attached to 2nd Battalion · died 6 October 1917, aged 21

Joseph Harding had already fought on the Somme before receiving a commission. He disappeared during the fighting at Passchendaele on 6 October 1917 and was later officially presumed dead.

Private, 2nd/4th Battalion East Lancashire Regiment, service no. 30167 · died 10 October 1917, aged 23

Lorne Ramshay Winthrop was killed in Belgium in October 1917. Official casualty records place him in the 2nd/4th East Lancashire Regiment and give 10 October as his date of death, while Brampton and family memorials preserve different unit and date details.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B85

Burial / commemoration Tyne Cot Memorial

Lance-Corporal Matthew William Holmes

Lance-Corporal, 6th Border Regiment · died 9 August 1915, aged 29

Matthew William Holmes served with the 6th Border Regiment at Gallipoli. First reported wounded and then missing in the Dardanelles, his fate remained uncertain for more than a year before his death was officially presumed.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B86

Burial / commemoration Helles Memorial · no known grave

Private Joseph William Warwick

Private, Durham Light Infantry · died 15 December 1917, aged 24/25

Joseph William Warwick served in the Durham Light Infantry and was killed in action at Ypres. Before the war he sang in St Martin’s choir and had won a gold medal for singing at the Carlisle Musical Festival.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B92

Burial / commemoration Oxford Road Cemetery, Belgium · grave V.H.15

Private Thomas William Potts

Private, 11th Border Regiment (Lonsdale Battalion), service no. 21450 · died 30 May 1916, aged 24/25

Thomas William Potts had been at the front for only about a month when he was killed on 30 May 1916. His officer described how the men had finished their work and were talking and smoking together when a German shell burst nearby.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B96

Burial / commemoration Authuile Military Cemetery · grave C.6

Private George Harold Burton

Private, Royal Fusiliers · died 17 August 1918, aged 19

George Harold Burton was only nineteen when he was killed near Albert, France. A newspaper report said he was shot by an enemy sniper while out on patrol; before enlistment he had attended Brampton County Secondary School and taken part in local sport.

Private Leslie Thompson

Private, Northumberland Fusiliers · died 10 June 1916, aged 25

Leslie Thompson was killed at his post during German shelling on 10 June 1916. He was the eldest son of a family formerly of Armathwaite and the husband of Bessie Thompson.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference B119

Sergeant John Patterson Aikman MM

Sergeant, Canadian Machine Gun Corps, service no. 68092 · died 13 April 1918, aged 32

Brampton-born John Patterson Aikman travelled from Mexico through Canada to enlist and reached France in August 1915. Twice commended, he received the Military Medal for gallantry at Vimy Ridge and was later buried back in Brampton Old Churchyard.

Brampton Old Church memorial reference C16 (military headstone) and C17 (family memorial)

Private, Machine Gun Corps (Infantry), service no. 13174 · died 30/31 October 1916, aged 20

Lance-Corporal, Border Regiment, service no. 19397 · died 18 June 1917, aged 28

Mervyn Eugene Wilson had already been wounded three times at Gallipoli, invalided home and returned to active service when he was struck by a shell while leaving the trenches in June 1917. Before enlistment he worked on the railway at Brampton.

Second World War

Flying Officer John Clifford North-Lewis

Flying Officer, Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve · died 2 May 1943, aged 29

Brampton Old Church memorial reference: A137

Burial / commemoration: Brampton Old Churchyard.

John Clifford North-Lewis was born on 22 July 1913, a twin son of the solicitor Henry North-Lewis and Eleanor North-Lewis née Bushell. His twin brother was William Esmond North-Lewis. John was educated at Harrow from 1927 to 1931 and entered Worcester College, Oxford, in October 1932. He gained a third-class degree in Jurisprudence in 1935 and entered the law as a solicitor.

He was commissioned Pilot Officer in the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve on 15 October 1941 and promoted Flying Officer in October 1942. In 1942 he married Margaret Taylor; his memorial describes him as the “adored husband of Margaret.”

On 2 May 1943, during a night-navigation training exercise from RAF Honeybourne, the aircraft in which he was flying developed serious trouble over the sea and crashed during an attempted forced return and landing. North-Lewis and two other crewmen were killed.

He was brought home and buried at St Martin’s, Brampton. He is also commemorated by Harrow and Worcester College.

Second World War

Sergeant Pilot James French Fearnley

Sergeant Pilot, Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve, service no. 999804 · died 30 November 1943, aged 23

Brampton Old Church memorial reference: None separately recorded · St Martin’s memorial N9

Burial / commemoration: Victoria (Royal Oak) Burial Park, British Columbia

James French Fearnley was born at Longtown on 9 June 1920, the son of Arthur and Anne Jane Fearnley. His father had served in the Northumberland Fusiliers during the First World War and was said to have survived being shot because a bullet was stopped by his paybook. Arthur later worked in the licensed trade; the family was associated at different times with the Globe at Longtown, the Blue Bell Inn in Carlisle and, by James’s death, the Bush Hotel at Brampton. James’s grandmother Agnes also lived with the family.

James attended Carlisle Grammar School from 1928 to 1937 and by 1939 was an art student. He joined the Royal Air Force Volunteer Reserve as Sergeant 999804 and undertook training in Canada.

On 30 November 1943 a Handley Page Hampden in which he was flying took off from RCAF Patricia Bay on a compass-related training exercise. In dense fog it struck wooded high ground near Ladysmith, British Columbia. Fearnley initially survived the crash and crawled clear of the wreckage. Civilian witnesses found him alive and able to speak, but he died from his injuries roughly an hour later; the other occupant died at the scene.

Fearnley was twenty-three. He is buried at Victoria (Royal Oak) Burial Park, British Columbia. His mother later travelled to Canada and visited his grave in 1950.
